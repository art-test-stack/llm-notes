Normalizing flows transform a tractable base density into a flexible data density through invertible transport. Finite or discrete compositions accumulate log absolute Jacobian determinants. Continuous normalizing flows instead integrate the vector-field divergence, equivalently the Jacobian trace, along an ODE trajectory. Both require tractable change-of-variables accounting, but not the same computation.

1. Change of variables

Let z ∈ ℝᵈ have a known density pZ(z), and let an invertible differentiable mapping produce

x = f(z), z = f⁻¹(x).

Probability mass is conserved. For a sufficiently small region around z, the linearization of f scales volume by the absolute Jacobian determinant:

dx = |det J_f(z)| dz, p_X(x) dx = p_Z(z) dz.

Substituting the first relation into the second and dividing by dx gives the density transformation:

p_X(f(z)) = p_Z(z) / |det J_f(z)| p_X(x) = p_Z(f⁻¹(x)) |det J_f⁻¹(x)|.

The two forms agree because Jf⁻¹(x) = Jf(z)⁻¹. In log space, the product becomes the quantity optimized in maximum-likelihood training:

log p_X(x) = log p_Z(z) − log |det J_f(z)|, z = f⁻¹(x).

For a composition f = fK ∘ ⋯ ∘ f1, determinants multiply and log-determinants add:

log |det J_f| = Σₖ log |det J_fₖ|.

Worked two-dimensional transformation

Take z = (z₁,z₂) from a standard two-dimensional Gaussian and define

x₁ = 2z₁ + 1 x₂ = z₁ + 3z₂.

The map is invertible, with z₁ = (x₁ − 1)/2 and z₂ = (x₂ − z₁)/3. Its Jacobian and determinant are

J_f = [2 0] [1 3], det J_f = 6.

Since pZ(z) = (2π)⁻¹ exp(−(z₁²+z₂²)/2), the transformed density is

p_X(x) = 1/(12π) exp(−½[((x₁−1)/2)² + ((x₂−(x₁−1)/2)/3)²]).

The factor 1/6 compensates for the sixfold expansion of area. This is the change-of-variables formula in a case where the Jacobian is triangular and the calculation is immediate.

2. Design constraints

  • Invertibility: every data point must map to exactly one latent point on the modeled domain.
  • Efficient forward transform: generating x = f(z) should be cheap when sampling speed matters.
  • Efficient inverse: evaluating data likelihood needs z = f⁻¹(x).
  • Efficient log determinant: a generic dense d × d determinant costs O(d³), so flows impose diagonal, triangular, low-rank, or otherwise structured Jacobians.
  • Expressivity: the structure that makes computation cheap must not prevent the composition from modeling useful dependencies.

No single architecture optimizes every property. Coupling flows favor parallel transforms, while autoregressive flows make one direction cheaper than the other.

3. Coupling flows

A coupling layer partitions its input into za and zb. One part passes through unchanged and conditions the transformation of the other.

Additive coupling

x_a = z_a x_b = z_b + t(z_a) z_a = x_a z_b = x_b − t(x_a).

The conditioner t need not be invertible. The full map is invertible because the unchanged partition is available when undoing the translation. Its block-triangular Jacobian has ones on the diagonal, so log |det J| = 0.

Affine coupling

x_a = z_a x_b = z_b ⊙ exp(s(z_a)) + t(z_a) z_a = x_a z_b = (x_b − t(x_a)) ⊙ exp(−s(x_a)).

Its Jacobian is triangular even though the derivatives of s and t may be complicated:

J = [I 0] [∂x_b/∂z_a diag(exp(s(z_a)))] log |det J| = Σⱼ s_j(z_a).

A RealNVP-style flow stacks affine coupling layers and alternates which coordinates are frozen. Permutations, checkerboard or channel masks, and learned channel mixing prevent the same subset from remaining unchanged. In image flows, invertible 1×1 convolutions provide a learned permutation across channels. The main limitation is that each layer directly transforms only part of the state, so depth and mixing are essential for expressivity.

4. Autoregressive flows

Autoregressive transformations make each coordinate depend only on earlier coordinates. This yields a triangular Jacobian, but the choice of conditioning variables determines which direction is parallel.

ArchitectureTransformationDensity evaluationSampling
MAFxᵢ = μᵢ(x<i) + σᵢ(x<i)zᵢFast: all zᵢ = (xᵢ−μᵢ)/σᵢ can be computed in one masked-network pass for an observed x.Slow: x₁,…,xd must be generated sequentially.
IAFxᵢ = μᵢ(z<i) + σᵢ(z<i)zᵢSlow: recovering z from an arbitrary x is sequential.Fast: given all base noise z, a masked network produces the parameters in one pass.

For both, log |det J| = Σᵢ log |σᵢ| in the direction written. Masked networks such as MADE zero selected connections so output i can depend only on permitted earlier inputs. They compute all conditionals in one network evaluation when the conditioning variables are already known; the dependency order still makes the opposite direction sequential.

Thus MAF is attractive when repeated likelihood evaluation is central, while IAF is attractive when fast sampling or a flexible variational posterior is central. They are closely related transformations viewed in opposite computational directions.

5. Spline flows

Affine transforms have limited local shape. A monotonic rational-quadratic spline instead divides a scalar domain into bins and maps each bin with a rational-quadratic function. A conditioner predicts positive bin widths, positive bin heights, and positive knot derivatives, commonly enforced with softmax and softplus parameterizations.

Monotonicity guarantees one-to-one behavior. The transform, derivative, and inverse are analytic within each bin; inversion reduces to locating the output bin and selecting the valid root of a quadratic equation. Linear or identity tails handle values outside a bounded interval.

Splines provide strong local flexibility—changing slope and curvature across bins—while preserving a cheap scalar log derivative. They can replace the affine part of coupling or autoregressive layers without abandoning tractable likelihoods.

6. Multiscale image flows

  • Squeeze: rearrange each 2×2 spatial neighborhood into channels, halving height and width while quadrupling channels. This is a volume-preserving reshape that exposes local pixels to channel mixing.
  • Split: factor out part of the representation at several resolutions. Later layers process fewer variables, and the factored variables receive conditional priors.
  • ActNorm: apply a learned per-channel scale and bias, initialized from the first minibatch. Unlike batch normalization, inference does not depend on batch statistics; the scale contributes directly to the log determinant.
  • Invertible 1×1 convolutions: multiply every spatial location by an invertible channel matrix. They learn channel permutations and contribute spatial-size times the matrix log determinant.

A Glow-style step combines ActNorm, an invertible 1×1 convolution, and an affine coupling layer. Steps are stacked inside a multiscale squeeze-and-split hierarchy. The design allows exact likelihood and parallel generation, but storing high-resolution activations and maintaining stable inverses can be expensive.

7. Dequantization

Image pixels are discrete integers, while standard flows define densities over continuous space. Fitting a continuous density directly at isolated observations is ill-posed as a model of discrete probabilities: density is not probability mass, and the objective can improve by placing arbitrarily narrow, high spikes on the observed lattice.

Uniform dequantization replaces an integer pixel vector y ∈ {0,…,255}ᵈ with

x = (y + u) / 256, u ~ Uniform([0,1)ᵈ).

The discrete probability assigned to y is the integral of the continuous density over its quantization cell. Training on uniform noise gives a variational lower bound on the corresponding discrete log-likelihood, up to the known scaling constant.

Variational dequantization learns a conditional noise distribution q(u|y). Its bound includes the entropy correction:

log P(y) ≥ E_q[log p(y + u) − log q(u|y)].

A learned q can shape noise around image structure and tighten the bound instead of forcing every quantization cell to use uniform noise.

8. Continuous normalizing flows

A continuous normalizing flow (CNF) replaces a finite stack of transformations with ODE dynamics:

dz(t)/dt = v_θ(z(t), t), z(t₀) ~ p₀.

Under suitable regularity, the ODE solution defines an invertible map: integrate forward to generate data and backward to recover the base point. The instantaneous change-of-variables formula is

d log p_t(z(t))/dt = −div v_θ(z(t),t) = −Tr(∂v_θ/∂z). log p₁(z(t₁)) = log p₀(z(t₀)) − ∫[t₀,t₁] Tr(∂v_θ/∂z) dt.

The divergence measures the infinitesimal expansion of volume. Computing the exact trace can scale poorly with dimension. Hutchinson estimation uses a random vector ε with E[εεᵀ]=I:

Tr(J) = E_ε[εᵀJε], J = ∂v_θ/∂z.

Automatic differentiation can compute the vector-Jacobian product without materializing J, trading exactness for estimator variance. CNF cost depends on the ODE solver's number of function evaluations: stiff or highly curved dynamics require small steps, and backward integration plus density tracking can be much slower than a fixed-depth discrete flow. Solver tolerances also affect numerical invertibility and likelihood accuracy.

9. Strengths and limitations

PropertyConsequence
Exact likelihoodFinite-layer flows provide exact change-of-variables likelihoods; CNFs provide likelihoods up to numerical integration and any stochastic trace estimate. Dequantized discrete data is trained through a bound.
Architecture-dependent speedCoupling flows and IAFs can sample in parallel; MAF sampling is sequential. MAF density evaluation is parallel; IAF inversion is sequential. CNFs pay adaptive solver cost in both directions.
Topological constraintsA standard equal-dimensional continuous bijective or diffeomorphic flow cannot tear connected support into disconnected pieces or change intrinsic dimension. Augmented, injective, surjective, manifold, discrete, and stochastic flow variants relax or change these assumptions with different likelihood, inversion, or sampling trade-offs.
Likelihood–quality mismatchHigh likelihood can reward low-level statistics or assign surprising scores to out-of-distribution data, so likelihood alone does not guarantee perceptually strong samples.
Numerical invertibilityExtreme scales, finite precision, spline boundary errors, ill-conditioned channel matrices, and ODE solver error can make nominal inverses disagree in practice.

10. Comparison

MethodCompared with normalizing flows
VAEsVAEs permit non-invertible encoders and decoders and optimize an ELBO with an approximate posterior. A lower-dimensional latent is a common design choice, not a defining property: VAEs may use lower-, equal-, higher-dimensional, or hierarchical latents, and compression depends on architecture and objective.
Diffusion modelsDiffusion learns to reverse a stochastic noising process, usually with many denoising steps and no architectural bijection. DDPM-style evaluation commonly uses a variational bound; score-SDE models can also estimate likelihood through the probability-flow ODE and continuous change of variables, subject to numerical integration, trace-estimation, and score-model error.
Energy-based modelsEBMs define an unnormalized score p(x) ∝ exp(−E(x)); their partition function and sampling can be intractable. Flows normalize by construction and sample by transforming base noise, but impose a structured invertible generator.
Flow matchingFlow matching trains a time-dependent vector field by regressing target velocities along chosen probability paths, avoiding likelihood-based ODE training and simulation during the training objective. At inference it still integrates an ODE and can produce a CNF; “flow matching” names the training method, whereas “normalizing flow” names the invertible density transformation family.
